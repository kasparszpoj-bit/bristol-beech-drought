# Field protocol: scoring crowns on Clifton and Durdham Downs

One page, for the phone. The trees, form and map are in the QField project
`qfield/beech_field_check/`. Walk date: 30 September 2026.

## The route

- **53 core trees (orange), numbered 1 to 53 from north to south:** tree 1 is
  by Westbury Park at the north end of Durdham Down; tree 53 is near
  Observatory Hill at the south end of Clifton Down. About 3 km end to end.
- **8 extras (blue), 54 to 61:** five sycamores and three beeches in St
  Andrews Park and Redland Playing Fields. Only if there is time.
- Scored trees turn grey on the map, so nothing is done twice.
- Allow about 2 to 3 minutes a tree: roughly 3 hours for the core, or two
  shorter walks (1 to 27, then 28 to 53).

## For each tree

1. **Find it.** Tap the point; the form shows the register species, trunk
   size and council asset ID. Record *Found where mapped*, *Found, position
   off* (more than about 5 m), or *Not found*. If not found, look for a stump
   and say so in the notes: a recently felled tree is itself a finding.
2. **Check the species** against the register.
3. **Stand back** about one to two tree heights, with the sun behind you if
   possible. Score the upper, sunlit crown. Ignore the shaded lower crown and
   old pruning cuts.
4. **Crown thinning (defoliation):** how much foliage is missing compared
   with a full, healthy crown of the same species. ICP Forests classes:

   | Class | Leaf loss |
   |---|---|
   | 0 none | up to 10% |
   | 1 slight | 11 to 25% |
   | 2 moderate | 26 to 60% |
   | 3 severe | over 60% |
   | 4 dead | |

   Tip: look for sky showing through the crown and for bare twig ends.
5. **Browning or yellowing (discolouration):** the share of the remaining
   leaves that are brown, scorched or yellow, in the same five classes. For
   copper beech, count leaves browned or crisped beyond their normal purple.
   Normal early autumn colour counts too: every tree is scored on the same
   day, so it affects all of them alike. Add a note if a tree looks like
   ordinary autumn colour rather than scorch.
6. **Dead wood in crown:** none, twigs only, branches, or large limbs.
7. **Early leaf fall:** none yet, some, or heavy (a carpet of fresh leaves
   under the tree).
8. **Nut crop (mast):** beech nuts, acorns, lime seeds or sycamore keys, on
   the tree or the ground: none, light or heavy. Heavy masting after drought
   is part of the story.
9. **Fungus at the base:** check the trunk base and the ground out to about
   3 m. Giant polypore: large tan rosettes of overlapping caps that bruise
   black. Otherwise none, other bracket, or unsure.
10. **Photo:** one photo of the whole crown, from the same side you scored.
11. **Notes:** anything odd: scorch on one side, bleeding on the trunk,
    storm damage, a tree marked for felling.

## Practical

- A power bank: GPS and the camera drain the battery.
- Keep off the roads that cross the Downs; score roadside trees from the
  grass side.
- The background map loads over mobile data. The trees and the form work
  without signal.
- Score honestly and consistently. The point is to test the satellite, so
  never look at the satellite results before scoring.
